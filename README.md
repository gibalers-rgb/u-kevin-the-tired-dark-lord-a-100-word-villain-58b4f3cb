## The entry

`main.ts` (Node 22, standard library only) writes one monologue of exactly
100 words to `/output/entry.txt`. The speaker is Malachar, a sorcerer-king
who has ruled for three centuries and now runs his empire mostly out of a
tax ledger. The tiredness is never announced — it lives in the pauses, the
deflections, and the scheme itself: letting the empire run itself for one
more winter. The sting lands in the last line.

The program self-checks before writing: the text must be 98–102 words and
1–600 characters (the gate), and it must contain none of the words that
would simply announce weariness (`tired`, `weary`, `exhausted`, `sleepy`).

## Run

```sh
node main.ts
cat /output/entry.txt
```

## The monologue

> Take the chair. The one... has a spring that bites. Sit. You've come to
> kill me, I know. The bards wrote your entrance — torches, rain, a sword
> with a name. I wrote it too, centuries ago, in the margins of a tax
> ledger, between the grain tariffs. The ravens I sent to watch the border
> came back asking for pensions. So. Here is my scheme, hero, since you
> insist, please: I shall... let the empire run itself for one more
> winter. Then — if you want the throne — you may have it. The paperwork
> will finish you yet.
